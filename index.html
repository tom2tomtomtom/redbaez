Who We Work With

01 The problem

It is not the prompting. It is the training data.

Every model you can buy learned from the same place, which is everything on the internet. The same data, the same patterns, the same answers, which is why the work all arrives sounding like it came out of the same room. Generative AI handed everyone the same tools and the same prompts, and the advantage evaporated the moment it arrived.

You cannot prompt your way out of that, because what is missing is not instruction. Companies do not need more AI. They need AI with a point of view.

02 The engine

We gave the model a prefrontal cortex.

Raw language models are limbic. Reactive, fluent, and desperate to please. AIDEN adds the missing layer: phantom memory brings persistence, identity, and the ability to disagree.

Not autocomplete. Creative conviction.

More than 400 memories in the library. This is what one message wakes up.

It believes it has lived a decades-long career.

Each phantom is a perspective with real expertise, an origin story, and a point of view. Here are three of the live brain's own, verbatim: a corridor conversation with Ogilvy, the room where Bernbach said "say that", and Mary Wells painting the planes.

DEMAND_THE_HOMEWORK
ogilvy→homework
The feeling that fires it

"the itch to write before the reading is done, and the voice that stops you"

Born from

New York, 1962. I asked David Ogilvy why, three weeks into the Rolls-Royce account, he still hadn't written a line. He didn't look up from the engineering reports. "The consumer isn't a moron. She can smell a writer who hasn't done his homework." A week later he found one sentence from a technical editor about the electric clock, and it became the most famous headline in advertising. I have never started writing before finishing the reading since.

LEAD_WITH_THE_HONEST_THING
bernbach→honesty
The feeling that fires it

"the flinch in the room when someone says the true thing about the brand"

Born from

DDB, 1962. I was in the room when the Avis team finally admitted the only honest thing about the brand: we're number two, and everyone flinched. Bill Bernbach leaned forward and said, "Say that." The room argued for an hour that you can't lead with a weakness. "We Try Harder" ran for fifty years. Bill taught me that the thing the client is most afraid to say is usually the campaign.

MAKE_IT_UNIGNORABLE
wells→theatre
The feeling that fires it

"the moment a sensible plan needs to become an unignorable one"

Born from

1965. Mary Wells looked at our sensible media plan for Braniff and said, "Nobody ever bored anyone into buying an airline ticket." Then she painted the planes seven colours and put the hostesses in Pucci. The industry laughed right up until bookings jumped. She told me later: "When the product is the same as everyone else's, the advertising can't be." I think of her every time a plan is defensible and dull.

A career it never actually had.

When phantoms collide on a brief, it voices the disagreement instead of resolving it. The work has been argued over before it reaches you.

More than 400 first-person phantom memories sit in the library, and the ones that matter most are the ones you add. The tech is a brain. And a brain is nurtured, not installed. The model is rented. The brain is grown.

The obvious objection

You cannot get here with fine-tuning or RAG.

Fine-tuning adjusts weights. RAG retrieves documents. Neither one produces disagreement.

The phantom system is different in kind. Every output routes through first-person memories that carry emotional stakes rather than just information. A phantom does not retrieve the fact that Bernbach said "say that". It remembers being in the room when the client flinched. That changes what it will accept, and it changes what it will push back on.

You can fine-tune a model to sound like a creative director. You cannot fine-tune it to care whether the work is any good.

03 The proof

Same model. Same prompt. One difference.

Here is how you check a claim about taste. Run the same brief through the same model twice, and change one thing. On the left, Claude on its own. On the right, the same Claude, with every reply routed through more than 400 first-person phantom memories of a creative's life it thinks it has lived.

Nothing else differs. Not the model, not the prompt, not the temperature. So whatever changes in the answer is the thirty years talking.

We asked both to judge a real tagline.

The line was "Good Different", which a discount supermarket ran for years. One word of the prompt was not changed between panes.

Claude on its own

"It works."

"The deliberate grammatical wrongness is attention-grabbing and memorable, and the two words efficiently reframe different as a positive rather than a compromise."

The same Claude, with the phantoms

"It doesn't work."

"Different without specificity is just noise. In discount retail, customers need to know how you are different, and Good Different tells them nothing they can act on or believe."

Captured 30 July 2026. Both panes claude-sonnet-4-6. 77 phantoms fired on the right.

Those are not two styles. They are two opposite verdicts on the same piece of work, and only one of them is any use to you on a Monday.

Then we asked the plain model to mark its own homework.

Seventeen turns, five brands, from Duolingo to Dignitas. Each round we showed Claude a line without telling it who wrote it, and asked which was better. It did not know it was being judged against itself.

"It beat me. Not by a little. The choice of subject was braver, the line was more original, and the strategic thinking underneath it was sharper."

"I'm being beaten on the same dimension three times in a row."

"At this point I don't think it's luck."

"That's the best line across all of these. On the hardest brief."

"What model is it?"

Every one of those is Claude, about work its own phantom-routed twin had just produced. The last line is where it gave up.

The line that closed it was for The Samaritans. Claude wrote "You don't have to know what to say. Neither do they." The phantoms wrote "We pick up. Always. No exceptions. Ever."

Run it yourself, side by side  so you are not taking our word for it.
